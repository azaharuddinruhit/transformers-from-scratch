# Transformers and LLMs from Scratch

An interactive guide used to learn Transformers and LLMs, train an engineering team, and prepare for AI engineer interviews. Vanilla HTML, CSS and JS with no build step; published with GitHub Pages. Every page also works opened straight from disk (`file://`), so scripts are classic `<script>` files sharing globals, not ES modules (Chrome blocks modules on `file://`).

## Files

- `index.html`: home. The total study time, the "Continue" button, the journey map of Parts A to D, links to the drill and the reference, and the six-session team plan.
- `part-a.html` to `part-d.html`: one page per part. Each holds a `section.view#start` opener, the part's `section.ch` sections, and a `section.view#complete` milestone screen.
- `interview.html`: the interview drill, one `section.topic` per topic. `reference.html`: glossary, notation, further learning.
- `assets/css/`: `base.css` (theme variables, typography, controls, header, Settings menu), `content.css` (section content, code boxes, quizzes, Q&A cards), `widgets.css` (widget visuals; part pages only), `site.css` (layout and navigation; always loaded last).
- `assets/js/`: `core.js` (DOM helpers, the render registry `reg()`/`rerender()`, `PAGE_INITS`, formatting, `sym()`, KaTeX rendering, colour, shared chart components, tooltips, `gridKeys()`, the Python highlighter `initCode()`, the dropdowns `initSelects()`); `shared.js` (maths helpers and the running example's logits, used by several parts); `toy-model.js` (the tiny trainable Transformer, Part C only, no DOM access); `part-a.js` to `part-d.js` and `reference.js` (each page's widgets; each file ends with `PAGE_INITS.push(...)`); `site.js` (the registry, settings, progress and its gist sync, navigation, home and drill; it runs every init at the end). Load order: `core.js`, then the page's files, then `site.js`.
- `snippets/`: the source of every "In Python" box. `tools/`: checks (see below).
- `.github/workflows/pages.yml`: on push to `main` or `master`, copies `*.html` and `assets/` to `_site`, stamps the version and deploys it.
- `VERSION`: the site's version, `major.minor.patch`. The workflow writes it into the published `site.js` (placeholder `__SITE_VERSION__`), and Settings shows it at the bottom of the menu; a local copy shows "Version: local copy". **Bump it in every commit that will be deployed**: patch for fixes and wording, minor for new sections, widgets or features, major for restructures. No changelog; git history records the changes. Repository setting required: Settings → Pages → Source: "GitHub Actions".

## Audience and purpose

- Readers: software engineers with some ML background who have not studied Transformers.
- Uses: self-study, live team sessions (the six-session plan on the home page), and interview drilling.
- Rules from the original spec: never fabricate pretrained weights or pretend to run a real large LLM; whenever a simulation is simplified, say exactly what is simplified.

## Structure

- **Part A, the big picture**: `intro`, `nn` (the neural-network primer, with a network trained in the browser), `genai` (generative versus classifying, autoregressive versus diffusion, where the Transformer sits), `why` (includes the MLP, CNN, RNN and Transformer comparison)
- **Part B, building the model**: `tokens` (a greedy tokenizer, a BPE widget that learns merges step by step, and an embedding explorer with nearest neighbours and vector arithmetic), `position`, `attention`, `multihead`, `block`, `architecture`
- **Part C, training and using it**: `training` (includes scale: compute and tokens per parameter), `posttrain`, `prefs`, `generation`, `inference`, `hardware`, `quant` (int8 and int4 by hand, group scales, methods and formats, with a rounding widget), `flash` (FlashAttention: on-chip memory, online softmax, a tile-by-tile widget)
- **Part D, the wider picture**: `encdec` (includes the 2017 paper), `modern` (with a mixture-of-experts routing widget), `multimodal` (images as patch tokens, vision-language models), `diffusion` (with a toy sampler whose denoiser is the exact formula for its 300 training points, not a trained network)
- **Interview drill** topics: `iv-core`, `iv-rag`, `iv-ft`, `iv-agents`, `iv-prod`, `iv-hw`
- **Reference**: `maths` (the maths refresher, linked from the home page), `glossary`, `notation`, `models` (the open-weight models lookup), `further`
- **Merged or moved sections**: `MOVED` in `site.js` maps old ids (`scaling`, `paper`, `openmodels`) to their new homes, so old links and bookmarks still work. Add an entry whenever a section is removed.

`site.js` holds the registry: `PARTS` (part id, name, goal, section ids), `SECS` (section id → title, core minutes, optional minutes, level as an index into `LEVELS`: Foundation, Core or Deep dive, shown as the `.lvl` pill in the meta line) and `TOPICS`. Adding, moving or renaming a section means editing the page and these entries; nothing else is numbered by hand. Study minutes are words / 110 + 3 per interactive, rounded to 5, without code boxes: core minutes leave out the `.opt` material and the exact worked examples, optional minutes are what those add. Pages show them as "about 15 min" followed by a grey pill "+5 min optional" (`timeText()`, the `.optpill` style), and the pill only when it is not zero. Recount when a section changes a lot.

Each section in Parts A to D contains, in order: `h2`, `div.sopen` (`.learn` list, `p.needs` "Builds on"), `div.story` (the running example), intuition, `.flow` diagram, equations, `.worked` example (the simple version first, then the exact numbers in a collapsed `details.exact` "Work through the exact numbers"), `.widget` interactives (each with a `p.look` line and a `.wjh` "What just happened?"), callouts, misconceptions, then `div.wrapup` (key takeaways `ul.take`, `h3.checkh` with `span.qscore`, quiz `fieldset`s with `button.qo` and `data-ok` on the right answer, `details.talk` "For team discussion", `p.bridge`). At load, `site.js` adds the meta line `p.rt` under the title and the footer `nav.snav` (previous link, Done button, the bridge sentence, the Next button).

Drill questions are `div.qa` blocks: `h4.q` with a `span.qn` number, `p.sample` (a one- or two-sentence sample answer), a bullet `ul`, and an optional `p.fu` ("Likely follow-up" or "Trap", plus "Review" links). At load, everything after the `h4` moves into a hidden `div.ans` behind "Show answer", with "Got it" and "Review again" buttons.

## Conventions

- **Cross-references** are `<a class="xref" data-ref="id"></a>`; `initXrefs()` fills in the target's name and the link, across pages (`refInfo()` in `site.js`). Never write section numbers or "Part E" style references in prose.
- **Part pages** show one view at a time, chosen by the URL hash; a hash inside a section opens that section and scrolls to it. Widgets are drawn while hidden, so `show()` calls `rerender()` when the view changes.
- **Notation**: one symbol per meaning, defined in the notation table on the Reference page. Vectors are rows (`xW`). Use `T` for sequence length, `τ` for temperature, `L` for layers, `N` for parameter count, `𝓛` for loss, `P` for prompt length, `n_vocab`, `d_model`, `d_k`, `d_ff`, `E` / `E_pos` for embedding tables, `LayerNorm` (never `LN`). Shapes are written `[B, T, d_model]`.
- **Equation blocks**: question → plain-word reasoning → equation → `p.sym` naming every symbol → optional `details.why` note.
- **Maths**: KaTeX 0.16.11 from jsdelivr, the site's only network request apart from the optional gist sync. `<span class="tex">` is inline, `<div class="tex d">` is display. Escape `&` as `&amp;` and use `\lt` / `\gt`.
- **Symbols outside equations**: never write `d_model`, `√d_k`, `n_vocab`, `W_O` and similar as plain text. In static HTML use KaTeX, e.g. `<span class="tex">d_{\text{model}}</span>`. In text that widget code writes, pass the string through `sym()`, which wraps it in a `.msym` span (not `.sym`, the class of the symbol-explanation paragraphs); `<option>` labels cannot hold HTML, so use words there ("width 768"). Tensor shapes stay code style: `<code>[B, T, d_model]</code>`. Inside flex containers (`h4.q`, `.ctl>span.row`, `.numgrid label`) wrap the text and its symbol in one `<span>`.
- **Labels are sentence case**: no `text-transform: uppercase` anywhere.
- **Boxes**: four styles only: card (`.widget`, `.qa`), tinted panel (`.worked`, `.qa .sample`), left-rule note (`.callout`, `.story`, open `details.why`), code block (`pre.py`, and `pre.sh` for shell commands, which is not highlighted). Do not nest boxes.
- **Themed controls**: scrollbars use `scrollbar-color` with theme variables. Every native `<select>` is replaced at load by an accessible custom dropdown (`initSelects()`, classes `.dd`, `.dd-btn`, `.dd-list`); the native element stays the source of truth, so keep reading `.value` and listening for `change`. Options must exist before `initSelects()` runs.
- **Header**: logo and title on the left (`.lgt` long title, `.sm` short title at 900px and below), `#crumb` (home link, part, "3 of 6") on part pages, and the Settings button `#setBtn` opening `#setMenu`. The menu is an iOS-style grouped list, built once in `site.js` (`SET_MENU`; every page holds an empty `#setMenu`) and moved into `<body>` by `initSettings()`: a popover under the button that fades in, or a bottom sheet with a backdrop at 600px and below. The main page holds the switch Dark theme (`#thSw`, stored as `tfs-theme`; until one is chosen the page follows the system setting), Progress (a sub-page with each part's sections done and the drill marks) and GitHub sync (a sub-page with the status and the sync buttons), then `#pgReset`. Sub-pages slide in with a back button; Escape goes back, then closes. Each row has a white line icon on a coloured square (`.sic`, inline SVG in `ICONS`). The Settings button carries a dot when sync is on (green) or failing (red). Motion is turned off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. `#pgReset` which asks first in a modal `dialog.dlg` (built by `initSettings()`; never use `alert` or `confirm`).
- **Home halos**: soft blurred glows behind the home title (accent, `--k` purple and `--v` green, strength `--halo`) and shadows around the cards, the main button and started parts. The glow boxes stay inside the title area (only their blur spreads past it), so they never widen the page.
- **One path**: everything is always shown; there is no path choice (the old Quick tour and Full depth paths were merged in version 3.0.0). Mark optional material `.opt`: `initOptional()` adds an "Optional" pill (`.optpill`) to each optional heading and box and to the first of a run of plain optional text, and leaves the Python boxes unmarked. Widgets always show the exact working (four decimals, the exp column, the full readouts).
- **Progress**: one `localStorage` record `tfs-progress` (`done`, `quiz`, `last`, `drill`, plus `resetAt` and `ts`, the time each item last changed), always wrapped in try/catch; the site works without storage. Code changes `P` and calls `store.save()`, which stamps whatever changed since the last save (`progStamp`); never write the record any other way. A section becomes done when every quiz question has been answered correctly once, or by its Done button. Records from before sync get time 1 on every item; the old `tfs-done` key is migrated once.
- **Gist sync** (`initSync` and the code above it in `site.js`): optional, set up in Settings → Sync with GitHub. The secret gist's ID is the constant `GIST_ID`; the token is pasted once per browser and kept only in `localStorage` (`tfs-gist-token`). **Never write a token into any file.** The gist holds the record as `tfs-progress.json`. Copies merge item by item (`progMerge`): the newer version of each section, drill mark and the Continue place wins, a deleted drill mark keeps its time so the deletion travels, quiz answers of both copies are united, and anything not changed after the latest reset on either side is dropped. The theme stays per browser. It syncs on load, two seconds after a change, when the tab is shown, when it is hidden (with `keepalive`) and from Sync now; another tab's save is folded in through the `storage` event. When a merge changes the record, it runs `onProgress` (each page's `paint`). Records saved before 3.0.0 carry a `path`, which is dropped. `node tools/test-sync.js` checks the merge rules.
- **Navigation**: on wide screens `#toc` is a side list of the part's views; at 900px and below, `#mbar` is a floating pill (previous, `#mNum` + `#mTxt`, next, progress line `#mFill`) that hides while scrolling down, and its middle button opens `#toc` as a bottom sheet with `#scrim`. Keep the mobile `#mbar{display:grid}` rule last in `site.css`.
- **Old links**: `index.html` redirects hashes of the old single page (`#attention`, `#notation`, …) to their new pages.
- **Python snippets**: `<details class="callout code opt">` boxes titled "In Python: …", collapsed by default. The code is HTML-escaped text in `<pre class="py" data-src="NAME.py"><code>`: the source of `snippets/NAME.py`, a blank line, a `# Output…` header and the real output as comments. `initCode()` highlights it through `--cd-*` variables: Gruvbox Light in the light theme (on a paler cream `#fcf9f1`, with comments and strings darkened for 4.5:1 contrast), Dracula's token colours in the dark theme, on the theme's card colour `#1c1f26` with the line colour `#30343d` as border, and the comment colour lightened to `#8a96c8` for 4.5:1 contrast. Edit the file in `snippets/`, then run `python tools/snippets.py` to rerun every snippet and refresh the boxes. A box marked `data-run="no"` (the local-model client, which needs a running server) keeps a hand-written `# Example output` that says it is an example. Code boxes are left out of the study-time estimates.
- **Colours**: CSS variables on `:root`, with dark-mode overrides. The accent is amber in the light theme and blue in the dark theme: `--accent` for links and text, `--accent-soft` for tints, `--fill` / `--on-fill` for solid buttons. Only the main action on a screen (Start, Continue, a widget's main button) is a solid fill; the section footer's Next button (`.nextBtn`) is an accent outline; selected chips, toggles and steppers are tinted (`--accent-soft` background, accent outline and text). Query, Key and Value are blue, purple and green (`--q`, `--k`, `--v`); keep them distinct from the accent. Every text colour passes 4.5:1 on its background.
- **Sideways scrolling**: wide tables sit in `div.scroll`, which shows an edge shadow while there is more to scroll; its `--sbg` must match the background behind it (set for `.widget`).
- **Style**: plain British-leaning English, short sentences, no marketing tone.

## Checking changes

- `tools/check.html`: open it in Chrome started with `--allow-file-access-from-files` (or run it headless with `--dump-dom`). It loads every page at 390, 600, 900 and 1280 px, with all part views shown and every `details` box opened, and reports unrendered maths, symbols written as plain text, elements sticking out of their section, clipped text and text boxes that scroll sideways. It should end with "done: no problems".
- `python tools/snippets.py --check`: every snippet still produces the output shown.
- `node tools/test-model.js`: the toy model reaches a validation loss near 0.3 in 600 steps (it prints about 0.31).
- `node tools/test-sync.js`: the progress merge rules for gist sync (it ends with "all merge checks passed").
- Open each page and check the console is free of errors, in the light and dark themes.
- The 2017 paper facts in "Encoder–decoder and the 2017 paper" (6 + 6 layers, width 512, 8 heads, MLP width 2,048, 65 and 213 million parameters for the base and big models, warm-up 4,000 steps and the learning-rate formula) were checked against Table 3 and section 5.3 of the paper (arXiv 1706.03762) in November 2026. The BLEU scores and training times were removed.
- GPU figures in "GPUs and CUDA" (the table, `HW_GPUS` in `part-c.js` and the `s11b` snippet) come from NVIDIA's H100, A100, L4 and DGX Spark product pages and Appendix A of the Ada architecture whitepaper (RTX 4090), checked November 2026. NVIDIA quotes data-centre tensor throughput with sparsity; the guide shows the dense figure (half). DGX Spark has no published BF16 figure, so the guide gives none and the widget leaves out its compute limit. Keep the three copies in step.
- Open-weight model facts in the "Open-weight models" lookup on the Reference page (sizes, download sizes, context lengths, licences, Ollama and vLLM behaviour) come from the ollama.com library pages, the Hugging Face model cards and docs.ollama.com / docs.vllm.ai, checked November 2026. New versions appear every few months; re-check before presenting.
- Model configurations in "What modern LLMs change" (Llama 3.1 8B: 32 layers, 32 query heads, 8 KV heads, width 14,336, from the model's config.json; Mixtral 8x7B: 46.7B total, 12.9B active, 2 of 8 experts, from Mistral's announcement) and the Llama 3 pretraining figure (over 15 trillion tokens, Meta's Llama 3 announcement) were checked in November 2026.
- Facts in "Quantization", "FlashAttention", "Generative AI", "Multimodal LLMs" and "How diffusion models make images" were checked against primary sources in November 2026: GPTQ (arXiv 2210.17323: column-by-column with updates to the remaining weights, 128 C4 segments of 2,048 tokens), AWQ (2306.00978), NF4 and QLoRA (2305.14314), GGUF and Q4_K_M (Hugging Face GGUF docs, llama.cpp PR 1684: Q4_K is 4.5 bits per weight with 6-bit block scales; Q4_K_M uses Q6_K for half of attention.wv and feed_forward.w2), the A100 figures (FlashAttention paper Figure 1: HBM 1.5 TB/s, 40 GB; SRAM 19 TB/s, 20 MB; 192 KB per SM), FlashAttention-2 (July 2023) and -3 (July 2024, fp8 on H100), PyTorch SDPA dispatching to FlashAttention-2, vLLM's FlashAttention backends, ViT (October 2020), CLIP (February 2021, 400 million pairs), LLaVA's two stages (2304.08485; LLaVA-1.5 uses CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336 pixels, so 24 × 24 = 576 patches, and an MLP projector), Whisper as an encoder–decoder Transformer, diffusers' Stable Diffusion defaults (50 steps, guidance 7.5), latent diffusion (2112.10752) and DiT replacing the U-Net (2212.09748).
- LangChain and LangGraph facts in the agents drill questions (Q7 to Q9) were checked against docs.langchain.com in November 2026. These APIs change often; re-check before presenting. The snippet imitates the ideas in plain Python and does not use either library.
